## How much do online teaching jobs in Delhi actually pay?

It depends heavily on whether you're doing freelance hourly tutoring or a salaried work-from-home role. On [Indeed](https://in.indeed.com/q-online-teaching-per-hour-l-delhi-jobs.html), a Home Tutor listing for Delhi is posted at ₹500–₹1,200 an hour depending on subject and grade level, while [Internshala's](https://internshala.com/jobs/teaching-jobs-in-delhi/work-from-home/) work-from-home teaching roles for Delhi NCR range from roughly ₹2.5 lakh to ₹7 lakh a year across its current listings.

The gap between those two numbers is mostly about commitment: hourly freelance tutoring pays more per hour but your hours can vary week to week, while a salaried role pays less per hour of actual teaching but comes with a fixed monthly income. Neither is objectively better — it depends on whether you want this as a side income or a full-time job.

## What qualifications do you actually need to teach online?

It depends on what you're teaching. School-subject tutoring — CBSE, ICSE, board exam prep — usually expects a relevant degree, and some structured-curriculum platforms specifically ask for a B.Ed.

If what you're teaching is a skill rather than a school subject — design, an instrument, fitness, a language, a trade — platforms and students generally judge you on a short demo and how clearly you can explain something, not a certificate. That's the gap Teach & Earn is built for: you don't need a teaching qualification to list a skill you're genuinely good at.

Government teaching roles (KVS, NVS, and similar) are a separate track entirely, gated by exams like CTET rather than a platform application — worth knowing if that's what you actually meant by "government online teaching jobs," since it's a completely different process from anything else on this page.

## Can you get an online teaching job in Delhi without experience?

Yes. A real share of Delhi NCR's work-from-home teaching listings on Internshala are explicitly marked "no experience required," spanning subjects from school-level academics to newer categories like AI training and coding for kids.

The trade-off is usually pay and selection — freshers tend to start at the lower end of the hourly range and get first pick of entry-level roles rather than senior faculty positions. That's true whether you're searching "for freshers," "for class 1 to 5," or "for class 6 to 10" — the fresher-friendly listings cluster around younger grade levels and newer subject categories, not board-exam coaching.

## How do you avoid wasting time on the wrong platform?

Watch out for platforms that charge you per "lead" before you know whether that lead is a real, paying student. It's a pattern we hear about often from trainers: you pay for a batch of leads, a chunk of them never actually convert into a paying student, and the fees keep coming regardless — which is exactly the kind of thing that quietly demotivates a genuinely good tutor out of teaching altogether, long before the pay issue does.

The fix isn't complicated: favour platforms that are free to join and only take a share once you've actually been paid by a real student, rather than ones that charge you upfront for access to unverified leads. If a platform only makes money when you do, its incentives line up with yours — that's the whole logic behind how Teach & Earn works, and it's worth checking for on any platform you're evaluating, not just ours.
