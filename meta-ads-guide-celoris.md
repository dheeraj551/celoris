# Meta Ads Masterclass: How to Run Facebook & Instagram Ads That Actually Convert (2026 Guide)

If you're running a business in Delhi/NCR — ya kahin bhi — chances hai aapke customers Instagram aur Facebook par scroll kar rahe hain right now. Meta Ads (Facebook + Instagram advertising) is still one of the most cost-effective ways to reach them, if you know how to set it up properly.

This guide breaks down everything from Ads Manager basics to reading your metrics like a pro — no jargon, just the practical stuff you need to launch your first (or next) profitable campaign.

## 1. Meta Ads Manager Walkthrough

Meta Ads Manager is your control centre for every ad you run across Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and the Audience Network. Before you touch a single campaign, you need three things in place:

- **A Facebook Page** for your business — this is your identity behind the ads
- **A Meta Business Manager (Business Portfolio)** account, set up at [business.facebook.com](https://business.facebook.com)
- **A verified payment method** added to your Business Manager

Once that's sorted, you can access Ads Manager directly at [facebook.com/adsmanager](https://www.facebook.com/business/tools/ads-manager), or through the mobile app on iOS/Android for managing campaigns on the go.

Meta structures every campaign in **three levels**:

1. **Campaign level** — your objective (Sales, Leads, Traffic, Engagement, etc.)
2. **Ad Set level** — your targeting, placements, and budget/bid strategy
3. **Ad level** — the actual creative (image, video, copy) the user sees

Getting this structure right from day one saves you from messy, hard-to-optimize accounts later. Meta's own [Beginner's Guide](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/help/1767727736592827) in the Business Help Centre is a solid reference if you get stuck at any step.

## 2. Audience Targeting and Custom Audiences

This is where most beginners either win big or waste their entire budget. Meta gives you three main targeting layers:

- **Core Audiences** — target by location, age, gender, interests, behaviours, and language. For a Delhi/NCR business, you'd start here: city/pin-code radius targeting + interest layers relevant to your niche.
- **Custom Audiences** — upload your existing customer list, website visitors (via Meta Pixel), or people who've engaged with your Page/Instagram. This is gold for retargeting warm leads who already know you.
- **Lookalike Audiences** — Meta finds new people who "look like" your best existing customers based on behaviour patterns. Great once you have at least 100+ conversions or a decent Pixel dataset to work from.

**Pro tip for local businesses:** don't go too broad and don't go too narrow. A 5–10km radius around your service area combined with 2–3 tight interest categories usually outperforms a massive, unfocused audience.

## 3. Ad Formats: Image, Video, Carousel, and Reels Ads

Different formats work for different goals:

- **Image ads** — fastest to produce, great for simple offers or announcements
- **Video ads** — higher engagement, ideal for storytelling or demonstrating a product/service in action
- **Carousel ads** — perfect for showing multiple products, course modules, or a step-by-step process in one ad
- **Reels ads** — short, vertical, native-feeling video that blends into the Reels feed; currently gets strong organic-style reach because Meta is pushing Reels inventory hard

If you're just starting, test at least two formats (say, one static image + one Reel) against the same audience to see what your specific audience responds to before scaling spend.

## 4. Budgeting and Bidding Strategies

You have two main budget types:

- **Daily budget** — average spend per day, gives Meta's algorithm flexibility to spend a bit more on high-performing days
- **Lifetime budget** — a fixed total spend across the whole campaign duration, which Meta paces automatically

For bidding, most beginners should stick with **Meta's automatic bidding** (letting the algorithm optimize for your objective) rather than manual bid caps — manual bidding is powerful but needs enough data and experience to use well.

**A simple rule for small businesses:** start with a modest daily budget (enough to get ~50 conversions/optimization events over a week per ad set), run it for at least 3–4 days before judging performance, and only then decide to scale, pause, or tweak.

Newer tools like **Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns** and **Advantage+ placements** use machine learning to automatically test audiences and placements — useful once you have some baseline data to feed the algorithm.

## 5. Reading Ad Performance Metrics: CTR, CPC, ROAS

Your dashboard will throw a lot of numbers at you. These three matter most:

- **CTR (Click-Through Rate)** — % of people who saw your ad and clicked it. Low CTR usually means your creative or hook isn't grabbing attention.
- **CPC (Cost Per Click)** — how much you're paying per click. Compare this against your average customer value to judge if it's sustainable.
- **ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)** — revenue generated for every rupee spent on ads. This is the number that tells you if a campaign is actually profitable.

Customize your Ads Manager columns to show these front and centre, and review performance **every 3-4 days minimum** rather than making decisions off day-one data — Meta's algorithm needs a short learning phase to stabilize.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Changing budgets/creatives too frequently (this resets the learning phase)
- Targeting too broad or too narrow without testing
- Ignoring the Meta Pixel/Conversions API setup — without it, you're flying blind on real conversion data
- Boosting posts instead of running proper Ads Manager campaigns for anything beyond simple awareness
